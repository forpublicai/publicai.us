Help decide what this is for

Before we build the public side of MOCSI, a statewide public process will decide what a free Maine AI and the open tools should do first, who gets priority access, and what the project must never do. The result is the mandate we're bound to. Any developer can promise community benefit. We'd rather Mainers define it. Sign up below to take part, host a session, or follow along.

What we're asking Mainers to decide

  • What a free Maine AI, built on library collections and public records, should be able to do first.
  • Which open tools for towns, libraries, schools, and state agencies come before the others.
  • Who gets priority access to the compute, and on what terms.
  • What MOCSI must never do. These become the red lines the mission lock enforces.

How it will work

The format is being designed now, and we'll publish it before the process starts. The shape: a representative group of Mainers drawn from across the state, sessions around Maine hosted in libraries and town halls, and an open online phase so anyone can weigh in. The results are published, including the objections. And it's meant to be run again on a set cadence, so the mandate is refreshed rather than fixed at founding.

What's in scope

Two things we'll say to participants up front, because a process that hides its limits is worse than none. In scope: the public-interest layer, all of it, including the red lines. Out of scope: whether the compute business exists. Objections to the whole idea are recorded and published all the same.

Sign up

Online sign-up is coming soon. Until then, email hello@publicai.co with the subject line "Public process sign-up", and tell us:

  • how you'd like to take part: as a participant, by hosting a session, by spreading the word, or by staying informed;
  • any organization you're part of; and
  • what you think a free Maine AI should do first.
